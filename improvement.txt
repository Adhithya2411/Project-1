================================================================================
AHRAG PROJECT — IMPROVEMENT RECOMMENDATIONS FOR RESEARCH NOVELTY
================================================================================
Prepared after full analysis of the codebase, corpus, evaluation suite,
router, embeddings, metrics, and RESEARCH_LIMITATIONS.md.

Focus: Dataset volume, training, evaluation rigor, and what needs to change
so you can make legitimate novelty claims in a research paper or thesis.
These are NOT feature requests — they are about making the existing system
provably better than baselines at scale.

================================================================================
1. CORPUS IS FAR TOO SMALL — THE SINGLE BIGGEST PROBLEM
================================================================================

CURRENT STATE:
  - 9 documents, 60 chunks, ~17 KB total text (~15,000 words)
  - All documents are hand-written specifically for this demo
  - Only 4 document types: policy, runbook, report, finance
  - Only 1 supersession chain (leave policy v1 → v2)
  - Only 1 restricted document (Q3 finance forecast)
  - Only 5 demo users with 5 roles

WHY THIS IS A PROBLEM:
  - At 60 chunks, ANY retrieval route finds almost everything. The README
    itself admits: "on a 9-document corpus almost everything is findable by
    any route." If every route ties, you cannot claim your router helps.
  - The router's governance-aware advantage (abstention on thin corpus)
    shows on exactly 2 out of 32 queries. That is not publishable evidence.
  - LSA fitted on 60 chunks has almost no co-occurrence data to learn from,
    so the embedding quality is artificially low.
  - BM25 on 60 chunks is basically a brute-force scan, not real retrieval.

WHAT YOU MUST DO:
  a) Scale to 500–1000+ documents, 5,000–10,000+ chunks minimum.
     This means the retrieval first-stage is genuinely selective and
     different routes will actually produce different results.

  b) Use REAL or REALISTIC data sources. Options:
     - EnterpriseRAG-Bench: ~500K synthetic company documents across 9 types
     - FinanceBench: 10,231 financial QA items with evidence strings
     - Company-10K: Publicly available SEC 10-K annual reports (real finance)
     - ConflictQA / HAGRID: Existing RAG benchmarks with citations
     - PolicyQA: Policy document question-answering dataset
     - Scrape real open-source policy documents from government websites
       (many are public domain), corporate handbooks published online, and
       technical runbooks from open-source projects

  c) Mix multiple REAL document types at scale:
     - 100+ HR/compliance policies with versioning chains
     - 100+ technical runbooks/SOPs from open-source projects
     - 100+ financial/business reports (SEC filings, annual reports)
     - 50+ project status reports and meeting notes
     - 50+ internal memos and communications
     This diversity is needed to make the doc_type_hints feature meaningful.

  d) Create MULTIPLE supersession chains (at least 10–15) so the freshness
     metric is not trivially 1.000 for all systems. Currently it tells you
     NOTHING because every system happens to lead with the current version.

  e) Create MULTIPLE restricted document classes with overlapping topics
     so the ACL boundary is genuinely tested. One restricted document
     is not a security evaluation.


================================================================================
2. EVALUATION DATASET IS TOO SMALL AND SINGLE-ANNOTATOR
================================================================================

CURRENT STATE:
  - 32 labelled queries total (only 25 answerable ones)
  - 8 query types, averaging only 3–4 queries per type
  - Single annotator who wrote both the corpus AND the labels
  - No inter-annotator agreement (IAA) metric
  - No statistical significance tests (the project itself says "no
    significance test is reported because none would be meaningful at n=25")
  - 0.03 difference in Recall@5 is well within noise

WHAT YOU MUST DO:
  a) Scale to 300–500+ labelled evaluation queries minimum.
     At n=300, a paired bootstrap test can detect meaningful differences.
     At n=32, you literally cannot claim anything statistically.

  b) Get a SECOND annotator (even a classmate) to independently label at
     least 100 queries. Report Cohen's kappa or Krippendorff's alpha as
     inter-annotator agreement. Without this, any reviewer will question
     your labels.

  c) Include adversarial/edge-case queries:
     - Ambiguous queries that could match multiple document types
     - Queries with misspellings of error codes (does R1 still work?)
     - Long, rambling queries that bury the key question
     - Queries that require information across 3+ documents
     - Queries where the superseded document is MORE lexically relevant
       than the current one (this is the real test of freshness handling)

  d) Use stratified evaluation with enough samples per stratum:
     - At least 30 queries per query type for statistical power
     - At least 20 permission-constrained queries across different users
     - At least 15 freshness-sensitive queries with competing versions

  e) Report confidence intervals (bootstrap 95% CI) on every metric
     comparison. Without these, your numbers are anecdotes, not evidence.

3. ROUTER IS RULE-BASED WITH HAND-TUNED WEIGHTS — NOT TRAINED
================================================================================

CURRENT STATE:
  - Router weights in config/router.yaml are hand-set by the author
  - The quality model is a linear combination of 23 features with manually
    chosen weights based on "reading of the literature's failure modes"
  - Weights were NOT fitted on ANY data — they are "unvalidated priors"
  - The research draft proposes a trained router but it was never built
  - ComplexityOnlyRouter (baseline B5) is the only ablation

WHY THIS IS A PROBLEM:
  - A rule-based router with hand-tuned weights cannot claim to be "adaptive"
    in the machine learning sense. Reviewers will say this is just a
    heuristic decision tree, not learned routing.
  - Without training, you cannot claim the weights are optimal or even good.
  - The utility function U(z|x) = Q - λ_L·L - λ_C·C - λ_R·R is elegant
    but the Q function is a linear model with guessed coefficients.

WHAT YOU MUST DO:
  a) TRAIN the router on real offline route labels:
     - Run all 5 routes (R0-R4) on each query in a training partition
     - Score evidence sufficiency against annotated gold references
     - For each query, assign the LOWEST-COST route that clears quality
       thresholds as the gold label
     - This gives you real training labels, not fabricated ones

  b) Train a proper ML classifier on the 23 features:
     - Gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost/LightGBM) — interpretable, fast
     - Small neural network classifier
     - Compare both against the current rule-based router AND against
       an LLM-based router (e.g., using GPT/Claude to classify query type)

  c) Use proper train/validation/test splits:
     - 60% train, 20% validation (for threshold tuning), 20% held-out test
     - The test set must NEVER be used to set any threshold or weight
     - Report results ONLY on the held-out test set

  d) Perform hyperparameter search on the lambdas (λ_latency, λ_cost,
     λ_risk) using the validation set. Currently they are hand-picked
     constants (0.09, 0.12, 0.55).

  e) Show learning curves: how does router accuracy change as training
     data increases from 50 to 100 to 500 to 1000 queries? This directly
     supports the claim that more data helps.

4. EMBEDDING BACKEND IS A WEAKNESS, NOT A FEATURE
================================================================================

CURRENT STATE:
  - Default is LSA (TF-IDF + truncated SVD) fitted on the 60-chunk corpus
  - LSA on 60 chunks has almost no co-occurrence data
  - Multiple failures are attributed to vocabulary mismatch that a neural
    encoder would fix (sem-003, sem-005 in the eval results)
  - sentence-transformers is optional but never actually evaluated
  - The README calls LSA a "deliberate" choice but the real reason is that
    the corpus is too small for it to matter

WHAT YOU MUST DO:
  a) ACTUALLY RUN the evaluation with sentence-transformers installed and
     compare the results side-by-side. The project is set up for this but
     nobody has done it. This is low-hanging fruit.

  b) Compare at least 3 embedding backends on YOUR larger corpus:
     - LSA (current default)
     - all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (the configured sentence-transformer model)
     - A larger model like all-mpnet-base-v2 or BGE-base-en
     Show that the router's R1-vs-R2 routing decision MATTERS more with
     better embeddings, because dense retrieval actually works differently
     from sparse when embeddings are good.

  c) On a large corpus, LSA will actually learn meaningful term associations.
     Show this empirically: LSA@60 chunks vs LSA@5000 chunks vs neural.

  d) Fine-tune embeddings on your domain (enterprise policies, runbooks).
     Even basic fine-tuning on in-domain data is a publishable contribution
     that shows you understand the embedding space.


================================================================================
5. STATISTICAL RIGOR IS MISSING
================================================================================

CURRENT STATE:
  - No significance tests anywhere
  - No confidence intervals on any metric
  - A 0.03 Recall@5 difference (0.840 vs 0.820) on 25 items is reported
    without any caveat that this is 1 item difference
  - No ablation studies beyond the fixed-route baselines

WHAT YOU MUST DO:
  a) Paired bootstrap significance tests on every pairwise system comparison.
     Report p-values. If p > 0.05, say "not statistically significant."

  b) 95% bootstrap confidence intervals on all key metrics:
     Recall@5, MRR, nDCG@10, Abstention Appropriateness, Citation Coverage

  c) Proper ablation studies that isolate each component:
     - Router WITH governance signal vs WITHOUT (already done: P1 vs B5)
     - Router WITH probe confidence vs WITHOUT
     - Router WITH freshness penalty vs WITHOUT
     - Router WITH risk model vs WITHOUT
     - Evidence gates WITH authority checking vs WITHOUT
     - Evidence gates WITH freshness enforcement vs WITHOUT
     Each ablation should show a MEASURABLE, SIGNIFICANT difference.

  d) Effect size reporting (Cohen's d) alongside p-values.
     Statistical significance alone is not enough — you need to show the
     effect is meaningfully LARGE, not just non-zero.

6. BENCHMARKING AGAINST PUBLISHED SYSTEMS IS NEEDED
================================================================================

CURRENT STATE:
  - Only compares against its own fixed-route baselines (B1-B4) and
    complexity-only router (B5)
  - No comparison against ANY published RAG system
  - No comparison against Adaptive-RAG (the paper cited as the premise)
  - No comparison against Self-RAG, CRAG, or other recent work

WHAT YOU MUST DO:
  a) Implement or reproduce Adaptive-RAG (Jeong et al., NAACL 2024) as a
     proper baseline. B5 is described as "the Adaptive-RAG premise" but it
     is a simplified reimplementation, not the actual system.

  b) Compare against at least 2–3 published baselines:
     - Adaptive-RAG (query complexity routing)
     - Self-RAG (self-reflective retrieval augmentation)
     - CRAG (corrective retrieval augmented generation)
     - A simple RAG pipeline (retrieve-then-generate, no routing)

  c) Run on at least ONE established benchmark dataset:
     - Natural Questions (NQ)
     - HotpotQA (multi-hop)
     - FEVER (fact verification)
     - Or a domain-specific benchmark like FinanceBench

  d) Show results on both YOUR custom dataset AND a public benchmark.
     Custom dataset alone is not enough for a paper — reviewers want to
     see you can compete on datasets others have used.

7. SPECIFIC DATASET EXPANSION PLAN (STEP-BY-STEP)
================================================================================

This is the actionable plan your professor is asking for:

PHASE 1 — Grow the corpus to 100+ documents (Week 1-2):
  - Collect 30+ real HR policy documents from public sources
    (government agency policies, university HR handbooks — many are online)
  - Collect 30+ technical runbooks from open-source projects
    (Kubernetes troubleshooting, AWS incident response, etc.)
  - Collect 20+ financial reports (SEC 10-K summaries, annual reports)
  - Collect 20+ project/status reports (open-source project reports)
  - Create 10+ supersession chains (old policy → new policy pairs)
  - Create 5+ restricted document classes with different ACL roles

PHASE 2 — Scale evaluation to 200+ queries (Week 2-3):
  - Write 25+ queries per query type (8 types × 25 = 200 minimum)
  - Get a second person to independently label 100+ queries
  - Include adversarial edge cases (misspellings, ambiguous queries)
  - Include cross-document queries that require 3+ sources

PHASE 3 — Train the router (Week 3-4):
  - Run all 5 routes offline on the 200+ training queries
  - Collect gold route labels based on evidence sufficiency scores
  - Train XGBoost/LightGBM classifier on the 23 features
  - Compare trained router vs rule-based router vs LLM router
  - Report on held-out test set with confidence intervals

PHASE 4 — Run comparative evaluation (Week 4-5):
  - Install sentence-transformers and run full evaluation
  - Implement Adaptive-RAG baseline properly
  - Run on FinanceBench or HotpotQA subset
  - Report all metrics with bootstrap confidence intervals
  - Generate publication-quality comparison tables and charts

8. NOVELTY CLAIMS YOU CAN MAKE AFTER THESE IMPROVEMENTS
================================================================================

After scaling up, you can legitimately claim:

  a) "Governance-aware routing outperforms complexity-only routing on
     abstention appropriateness by X% (p < 0.01, n=300+)"
     — Currently this is a 0.031 difference on n=32, unpublishable.

  b) "The proposed router matches always-maximal retrieval quality while
     reducing estimated cost by Y% on a Z,000-chunk enterprise corpus"
     — Currently this is a 1.7% cost difference on 60 chunks, meaningless.

  c) "The trained governance-aware router achieves W% route selection
     accuracy vs V% for the rule-based version, demonstrating that
     offline route labels improve routing decisions"
     — Currently there IS no trained router; this is the open hypothesis.

  d) "ACL pre-filtering as a hard architectural constraint (not a utility
     term) provably prevents information leakage across N adversarial
     query types on a corpus of M restricted documents"
     — Currently tested on 1 restricted document and 4 ACL queries.

  e) "Freshness-aware evidence packing correctly prefers current versions
     over superseded ones in P% of freshness-sensitive queries across
     Q supersession chains"
     — Currently: 1 supersession chain, metric is trivially 1.000 everywhere.

9. ADDITIONAL THINGS REVIEWERS / PROFESSORS WILL ASK ABOUT
================================================================================

  a) REPRODUCIBILITY: Set a random seed for everything. Document the exact
     hardware (CPU/GPU, RAM), Python version, and package versions used.
     Provide a single script that reproduces all results end-to-end.

  b) LATENCY AT SCALE: Currently everything runs in ~1ms because the corpus
     is tiny. At 10,000 chunks, R4 (decomposed iterative) should genuinely
     take longer than R1 (sparse BM25). This is when the cost-efficiency
     claim becomes real and measurable.

  c) HUMAN EVALUATION: For at least 50 queries, have 2 human judges rate
     answer quality on a 1-5 Likert scale. Report agreement and average
     scores per system. Automated metrics alone are not convincing for
     answer quality.

  d) ERROR ANALYSIS: For every system, categorize its failures:
     - What fraction of errors are retrieval failures?
     - What fraction are routing failures (wrong route chosen)?
     - What fraction are generation failures?
     - What fraction are ACL/governance failures?
     This shows you understand WHERE and WHY your system fails.

  e) REAL LLM EVALUATION: Run with Anthropic/OpenAI in the loop and
     measure RAGAS faithfulness, answer relevancy, and context precision.
     The extractive generator makes faithfulness trivially perfect but
     that is testing the generator architecture, not the retrieval.
