When Croke Park was first used for the Railway End of the park was little more than a mound of earth.  Its name was originally called Hill 60.  That original name came from a hill in Gallipoli on which the Connaught Rangers suffered heavy casualties in late August 1915.  Contrary to common belief, the Royal Dublin Fusiliers did not participate in this battle, although the latter regiment did lose heavily during the wider Gallipoli campaign (most notoriously at 'V' Beach, Cape Helles, the previous April).
