Roger David Casement (1 September 1864 – 3 August 1916), formerly known as Sir Roger Casement  between 1911 and shortly before his execution for treason, when he was stripped of his knighthood and other honours, was a British civil servant who worked for the British Foreign Office as a diplomat, and later became a humanitarian activist, Irish nationalist, and poet.  Described as the "father of twentieth-century human rights investigations", he was honoured in 1905 for the Casement Report on the Congo and knighted in 1911 for his important investigations of human rights abuses in Peru.  He then made efforts during World War I to gain German military aid for the 1916 Easter Rising that sought to gain Irish independence.
